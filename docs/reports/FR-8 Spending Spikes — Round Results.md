## Setup

- **Data:** the default dataset from `main` (360 users, schema 4).
  - There are 240 train users in 5 user-grouped folds, stratified by persona, with 411 planted monthly spikes (373 `clear`) in 88,232 periods after the warm-up.
  - Categories start at each user's first purchase in them, and only complete months are scored (milestone 1, after review on #59).
  - Validation season profiles come from train users only, and the leak check rebuilds them from the data to confirm it.
- **The common operating point:** in each held-out fold, a run flags its top 0.035 eligible periods per user-month. Months the label contract ignores stay in the budget, as a deployed cutoff can't know them.
- **The product rules apply to every run:** spend ≥ 1.3× usual, and at least 2 purchases in a usual month (decision 1, enforced by the contract).
- **Candidates** (`configs/experiments/spending_spikes/`), each in `SpikeThresholded(precision=0.80)`:
  - κ is searched over {0.5, 1, 2} within each fold on its training users (decision 14). The final fits chose **κ = 2**, the top of the grid, for both candidates.
  - On the final fit, search recall was 0.474, 0.513 and 0.523 for the Poisson, and 0.470, 0.504 and 0.518 for the negative binomial.
  - β, fitted without labels, is 0.24 for both. The negative binomial's pooled over-dispersion is 0: most users' trailing variance is at or below their mean.
- **Time:** 3.8 minutes for all six runs on a laptop CPU. Scoring itself takes milliseconds (`latency_batch_ms` 4–6 ms for 200 periods); the rest is loading data, building season profiles and the leak check.

## Reading the results

- **The negative binomial and the Poisson are the same model here.** The fitted pooled over-dispersion is 0, so the negative binomial differs only where a user's own trailing variance exceeds their mean, shrunk by their months of history. That's what decision 6 anticipated: the tie goes to the variant that will hold on over-dispersed real counts.
- **Seasonality doesn't help out of fold, and that's informative, not a bug.** The POC credited it with 1–4 points, but the POC's profile pooled every month, later ones included. Built as of each month, no profile exists in 2024 (35% of scored periods). After that, one earlier year of the user's own is a noisy estimate, which is why κ = 2 (more weight on the profile) wins the search. Precision at the rate doesn't fall without it either (0.798 against 0.794). It stays in the candidates as designed; whether it earns its place on real data is a v2 question.
- **κ landed on the edge of the grid.** A larger κ might do slightly better. The grid was decided before the round (decision 14), so it isn't widened here.
- **Where the misses are:**
  - **low volume:** recall is 0.07 in categories with fewer than 4 purchases in a usual month, 0.43 at 4–15 and 0.76 at 16+. Groceries, at about 1.2 purchases a week, is lowest (0.29). The oracle misses most of these too (FR-8 Feasibility);
  - **freelancers:** recall is 0.30 against 0.60–0.66 for the other personas, read only and never gated. Their income swings make an ordinary busy month look like a spike, and the pooled β (0.24) is half their true elasticity. A per-user elasticity was deferred (open question 2).
- **False positives spread over categories and months.** August (11) and January (9) lead slightly. Thirteen are in categories with fewer than 4 usual purchases.
- **Excess coverage** of the 5 largest charges is 0.50–0.52 on flagged true spikes, below the POC's 0.69 over all spikes. Flagged spikes have more purchases, so 5 charges cover less of them. "Usual" is off by a median 8.9% against the generator's expected spend.
