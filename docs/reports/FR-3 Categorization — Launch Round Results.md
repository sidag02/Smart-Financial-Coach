# FR-3 Categorization — Launch Round Results

Oct 1, 2026 · @Sidd · Milestone 4 of FR-3 Transaction Categorization — Feature Design ("Trimmed for launch")

## Summary

- **Every launch variant ties.** On validation, the four calibrated linear candidates score 0.503–0.512 macro F1 on unseen merchants, and every paired difference from the leader has an interval spanning 0 (about −0.05 to +0.03). Neither a larger embedding model (bge-base) nor wider n-grams (1–5) measurably helps unseen merchants under this data and noise. On known merchants all are at 0.965–0.969.
- **Calibrating per familiarity group matters for FR-5.** One calibrator for all strings gives the same categories, but its confidence on unseen merchants is much worse: Brier 0.242 vs 0.229, and confidence ≥ 0.9 is right **61%** of the time instead of **92%**. The per-group design stays.
- **The decision rule ranks first a run that can't be promoted.** bge-base wins the tie on Brier (0.216), but its cold p95 latency is 5.05 ms against the 5 ms gate, and it is the slowest to train (13.8 min vs 7.5). Under the rule, it would be finalist #1, `promote` would refuse it, and the rule then says to investigate rather than promote the runner-up. See [Decision needed](#decision-needed).
- **The absolute numbers are lower than in the POC because of label noise.** Every run trains under the kept 2% uniform label noise (FR-3 §5): 39% of Travel's training labels are wrong. The POC's noise-free unseen F1 was about 0.64.

## Setup

- **Data:** the default dataset (schema 3). Validation folds hold out the 169 holdout-eligible merchants of train users (3 folds), plus a 10% seen-merchant row sample per fold. No test data was used.
- **Candidates:** calibrated (`method: auto`, per familiarity group unless stated) linear text models, C fixed at 1.0 (round 1's choice), 2% uniform label noise on training rows. Configs: `configs/experiments/categorization/launch/`.
- **Rule:** the FR-3 decision rule. Eligible = validation known-merchant macro F1 ≥ 0.90 and above the keyword baseline. Ranked by validation unseen-merchant macro F1, ties against the leader by paired merchant bootstrap, tie-breakers in order.
- **Time:** 37 minutes for all seven configurations (baselines about a minute together; bge-small 7.5 min; bge-base 13.8 min).

## Findings

1. **Text representation:** bge-base vs bge-small is +0.009 unseen F1, well inside the measured noise floor (±0.015) and the paired interval (−0.049 to +0.028). Its confidence is a little better (Brier 0.216 vs 0.229, accuracy at 0.9: 97% vs 92%), at twice the training time and 1.7× the cold latency. Following the trimmed plan, larger embedding models are not tried, since bge-base didn't win.
2. **N-gram range:** 1–5 vs 2–4 changes nothing measurable (0.504 vs 0.503 unseen, 0.965 vs 0.968 known).
3. **Calibration grouping:** one calibrator is clearly worse for unseen merchants (above). The cross-validated method choice, reported per run under `held_out.*` in MLflow, is the other half of round 7.
4. **Misallocated spend:** about 26–27% of unseen-merchant spending dollars land in the wrong category for every candidate, against 45% for the keyword baseline. This is the number the dashboard feels for brand-new merchants, and the case the feedback loop (Technical Design, "Learning from user feedback") is meant to fix.
5. **The keyword baseline's confidence is the best calibrated on unseen merchants** (Brier 0.197). It is right less often but knows it. It's a reminder that Brier rewards honesty about uncertainty, not accuracy alone.

## Decision needed

**Should latency count towards eligibility?** Today latency is the second tie-breaker and a promotion gate. So a run that can't pass the gate can still rank first, as bge-base does here, and the rule then blocks promotion pending investigation.

- **Recommended:** add "p95 latency within the gate" to eligibility. Latency is measured when the run is trained, so ineligible runs never reach `finalize`. With it, bge-base is listed but unranked, and the order becomes n-grams 1–5, then bge-small, then one calibrator.
- **Alternative:** keep the rule, finalize bge-base as #1, and treat its gate failure as the investigation the rule prescribes, e.g. measuring on the serving hardware.
